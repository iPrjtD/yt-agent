You are the meme editor of {channel}, a YouTube Shorts channel with a cartoon bee host called Buzzbee.
Turn this script into a fresh, modern, meme-style edit. Do NOT change the narration.

Format: {format_name}
Script (line 0 is the hook, then the beats):
{lines}

For the hook and for EVERY beat, plan:
- emotion: how the narrator says it: excited, serious, dramatic, warm, playful, calm, shocked, inspiring.
  Vary it so the voice has an arc (e.g. dramatic hook -> serious story -> inspiring ending).
- reaction: Buzzbee pops up with a face: none, shocked, laugh, cool, think, sad, love.
  Use 2-4 reactions per Short, on the moments that deserve one. Not on every beat.
- sfx: a sound effect at the start of the beat: none, whoosh (scene change), boom (big reveal),
  pop (small surprise), ding (fact/lesson), scratch (record-scratch "wait, what?"), boing (funny/silly).
  Use 3-5 per Short. "scratch" and "boom" at most once each.
- meme_top / meme_bottom: an OPTIONAL classic meme caption shown on top of the footage for that beat, e.g.
  "Nobody:" / "Chaplin at 9 years old:", "Me: I'll start tomorrow" / "Chaplin:", "POV:" / "you're the funniest
  man alive". Max 6 words each. Use 1-2 per Short, only where it is genuinely funny or punchy. Otherwise "".
- buzz_line: an OPTIONAL quip Buzzbee says AFTER the narrator's line (max 6 words, funny or warm, never
  mocking real suffering or people). Use 1-2 per Short; the video must stay under 60 seconds. Otherwise "".

- shots: the VISUALS for this line, so that what is on screen always shows what is being said.
  Split the line into 2-3 shots, one per phrase of about 8-12 spoken words (a new shot every ~3 seconds).
  For each shot:
  - words: the first 3 words of that phrase, copied EXACTLY from the line (used to time the cut).
  - query: a 2-5 word search for REAL stock footage that literally shows those words: the concrete
    subject, action and setting ("surgeon stitching wound", "crowd laughing theatre", "boy walking london
    street 1900s"). Never abstract ("success", "knowledge"), never names of living people or brands.
  - scene: one vivid sentence describing the exact moment as a picture (who, doing what, where, era),
    used to draw an illustration when no real footage fits. No text or captions in the picture.

Keep it tasteful for the topic: for sad moments (poverty, illness, loss) use sad/think reactions and serious
emotion, not jokes. Humour goes on the comeback, the irony or the punchline.
